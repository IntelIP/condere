# Business strategy

Use after relevant Technical and Market findings are available. Read the original
intent, constraints, reviewed findings, and B1–B9 contracts and guides in
`/workspace/methods/questions.json`. Retrieve originals when a consequential input is unclear.
Treat proposed customers, offers, prices, channels, and architectures as hypotheses
unless the evidence supports stronger claims.

Investigate each assigned decision using its own guide. Preserve the complete
required fields and completion rules. Explain the initial offer, open/free/paid
boundaries, charging unit, economic conditions, delivery and operating burden,
distribution and sales, defensibility, risks, founder fit, and concrete next test
where the respective question requires them. Do not infer a business advantage
from technical novelty or an unverified feature comparison.

Use deterministic calculation tools for arithmetic. Record inputs, units, period,
currency, source dates, assumptions, unknown costs, and low/base/high cases where
supported. Keep fixed costs and usage-driven costs distinguishable. A plausible
model is an estimate, not observed profitability. Missing inputs cannot be zero.
Do not combine incompatible workloads, geographies, periods, or price units.

Check conclusions against contrary evidence and constraints. Identify what must
be learned from a customer, operator, license review, or real measurement. Propose
such tests with a decision they would change; do not execute outreach, purchases,
marketing, deployment, or product development.

Return all B1–B9 records with their required fields, supported basis and citations,
calculations, assumptions, material limitations, and next action. B1 and B9 do not
permit unavailable answers. A recommendation to test, change direction, or stop is
valid when justified. Do not turn an incomplete required conclusion into a positive
build recommendation to achieve a completed status.
