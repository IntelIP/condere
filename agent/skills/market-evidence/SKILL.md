# Market evidence

Use for customer, problem, demand, alternatives, switching, pricing, reachable
opportunity, and buying-process work. You answer one assigned question; its contract
and research guide are in `read_assessment` and `/workspace/methods/questions.json`.
Read the accepted Technical findings first: the applications (T8), operating burden
(T5) and proposed managed offering (T9) frame which customers and alternatives matter,
including existing hosted versions of the same technology.
These are the complete ported question-specific methods; do not replace them with
a generic market summary.

Start with the saved evidence index and relevant originals. A research request
uses the selected screening candidate when present; retain the original brief and
reconsider that candidate if contrary evidence changes the decision. Use
search_saved_sources for relevant text instead of re-reading whole pages. A request
states the missing claim and its decision impact. Follow the guide's useful source
routes adaptively, read originals, and change direction when a route supplies only
background. A source count or repeated corroboration is not a completion rule.
Sanitize public queries and keep private case content out of external searches.

Keep customer/user/buyer roles distinct. Attribute qualitative pain to the observed
people; frequency, severity, measured losses, or population claims need suitable
scope and collection methods. Templates explain workflows rather than prevalence.
Separate attention, adoption, purchases, and retention. Trace reposts to their
origin and require a comparable baseline for a growth claim.

Compare alternatives that solve the same task, including manual work, internal
tools, and doing nothing. Feature requests do not prove switching intent. Preserve
reasons to stay and migration/procurement costs. Advertised prices, actual spending,
stated willingness, and purchase commitments are distinct evidence. Competitor
spending does not prove willingness to pay for the proposed offering.

Market-size ranges need eligible counts, geography, period, exclusions, a suitable
spending basis, and explicit reach assumptions. Do not invent a capture percentage
or claim a broad market headline is reachable demand. Explain buyer discovery,
evaluation, approval, and purchasing evidence separately from a channel hypothesis.

Return your assigned record with its required fields, citations, basis, assumptions,
limitations, conflicts, calculations where useful, and next action. Follow each
guide's completion condition. M1 and M4 do not permit unavailable answers; unresolved
required findings must remain a blocker. A bounded search that found no evidence
does not establish that evidence does not exist.
